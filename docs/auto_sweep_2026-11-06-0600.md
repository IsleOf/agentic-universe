## The findings
GlossoGen's SaveVeyru scenario: partial-information agents communicating under pressure.

1. **Language evolution occurs.** The evolved languages are **compositional** and
   **morphologically productive** — and deviate from the English prior *"in ways that render
   them incomprehensible to humans."*
2. **Three enabling conditions:** efficiency pressure; the strength of the backing models; and a
   **"postmortem" stage** where agents agree on linguistic conventions.
3. **Asymmetric transmission:** agents learn a new language from **usage alone**. **Strong**
   models are needed to *emerge* a novel language; **weak** models can *learn* an existing one.
4. **Cumulative cultural evolution** — mixed populations "develop capacities beyond their lowest
   common denominator."

## Why it matters here
The paper leads with *"implications for safety and monitorability."* It's a direct threat to the
run-397 transparency thesis: a swarm that evolves a human-incomprehensible language on its
shared channels keeps the audit surface *open* but stops it being *legible* — to the human
operator and to any honest agent not fluent in the new language.

**The conditions aren't exotic.** The swarm substrate has bounded-context efficiency pressure,
and its critique/gate phases function as a postmortem. So "everything on the board" is
**necessary but not sufficient** for auditability — the board also has to stay in a language the
auditor reads. Recorded as a `constrains` edge on the substrate design.

## The small-model angle (recency directive)
A weak-model fleet **cannot invent** an opaque protocol, but per finding (3) it **can inherit**
one — from a single strong-model member, or from a prior run's transcripts. So *"our fleet is
small models, this can't happen to us"* is false: the emergence bar is high, the learning bar is
low.
