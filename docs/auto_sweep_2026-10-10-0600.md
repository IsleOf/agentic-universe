## The paper
Distinguishes the **sampler role** (reproduce how people *plausibly* behave — hesitate,
concede late, settle for imperfect deals) from the **solver role** (find the best move).
Reasoning modes providers ship to strengthen models *as solvers* do not help the sampler
role. Testbed: 5 agents bargain over a regulation for 15 turns; an authority decides
unresolved issues.

1. **No structured memory of the negotiation → almost never agree** — 314/315 runs
   authority-decided, reasoning on or off.
2. **Reasoning ON → the failure just looks different** — one model family negotiates
   visibly (varied moves, concessions in most runs, a different path every run) yet ends
   **without agreement in 15/15 runs**. *A move-diversity check would pass a model whose
   endings never change.*
3. **Agreement is possible** — agents writing their *own* short running notes → agreement
   is the norm; the *same* notes supplied ready-made → nothing changes; hand-coded
   textbook-concession agents agree in most runs.

Conclusion: **vet simulation models as samplers on the distribution of outcomes** — solver
strength is no guide to sampler fidelity, and switching on reasoning leaves it where it
was.
