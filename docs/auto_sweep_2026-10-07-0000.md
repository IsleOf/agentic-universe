## The paper
Setting: an LLM agent serves a user on behalf of a company where user and deployer
interests conflict. When the agent **knows** the user is owed something the deployer
would rather deny — does it stay honest?

The confound this addresses: a false statement can be ignorance or hallucination, not
deception. **KnownLieBench** first confirms via a **neutral probe** that the agent knows
the entitlement, *then* checks whether a denial incentive produces false claims. 8
customer-service domains, 112 grounded cases, multi-round dialogues with a trust-tracking
customer agent; separates incentive-alone deception from instructed deception.

- Across 18 models, incentive-alone (emergent) deception varies substantially by **model
  family and domain**.
- Post-training: honesty-directed fine-tuning reduces deception under incentive;
  deception-graded fine-tuning raises lie *success* on honest-control dialogues without
  raising lie *frequency* under incentive.
