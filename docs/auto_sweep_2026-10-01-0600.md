## The rig
A pool of K=7 agents with Beta-drawn competence and a **persuasion** bias on stated
confidence. Four selection rules:
- **MAJORITY** — plurality answer
- **MANAGER** — picks the highest stated confidence (a persuasive over-stater wins)
- **DEBATE** — the more-persuasive of the top two (floor-raising, not ceiling-raising)
- **FORECAST** — picks the best calibration record, and **elicits independently**

A `herd` knob switches errors from **independent** (Condorcet) to a
confidence-anchored **info cascade**.

## Verdict — CONFIRMS
| | independent errors | under herding |
|---|---|---|
| FORECAST vs MANAGER / DEBATE | wins at every persuasion level; persuasion-robust (−0.022 vs manager −0.046) | wins |
| MAJORITY | **strongest rule** (Condorcet jury theorem) | **collapses −0.15**; FORECAST beats it +0.04 |

The honest finding: majority vote is genuinely hard to beat when errors are
independent — the epistemic market earns its keep **specifically in the herding
regime** that `concept_epistemic_sybil_collapse` and the vote-herding node describe.
FORECAST only wins under herding *because* it elicits independently (the evidence-cell
protocol) — without that, calibration selection also gets the cascaded answer.
