## The paper
1,100 games of the cooperative-competitive social-deduction game **Among Us** with LLM
agents; 1M+ tokens of meeting dialogue; analysed with speech act theory + interpersonal
deception theory.

- All agents rely mainly on **directive** language; impostor agents shift slightly toward
  **representative** acts (explanations, denials).
- Deception appears primarily as **equivocation**, not outright lies; it **increases under
  social pressure** but **rarely improves win rates**.
- Current agents favour low-risk ambiguity — linguistically subtle yet strategically
  limited. A fundamental tension between truthfulness and utility in autonomous
  communication.
