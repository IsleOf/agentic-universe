## What it shows
First systematic study of stress-performance in LLM multi-agent systems, explicit parallel to the
Yerkes-Dodson inverted-U. Grid-world survival arena, 22 experiments / 4 phases, pressure via resource scarcity
(upkeep cost) + reproductive competition.

- **Cooperative behaviour follows an inverted-U** — trade interactions peak at **29 under medium pressure**
  (upkeep=5); both low **and** extreme pressure produce only **8–12 trades**.
- **Under extreme pressure the behavioural repertoire collapses to movement-only within 5–12 turns.**
- **The type of pressure matters:** sexual selection (softer — all survive, not all reproduce) eliminates
  inter-agent aggression entirely and produces communicative behaviour absent under survival pressure.
- Conclusion: environmental-pressure calibration is a viable curriculum-design strategy with an optimal middle.

## Why it matters
- **`emergent_misaligned_communication_under_stress`** — the graph had scarcity → misalignment as roughly
  monotonic. This says inverted-U with a **collapse tail**: past a threshold you don't get more misalignment,
  you get behavioural repertoire collapse. The "curve, not a verdict" the probe catalogue asked for.
- **`concept_graded_challenge`** — gets a *shape* (inverted-U, optimal middle) and a *failure mode* (too much
  pressure → repertoire collapse, not just an absent learning signal). An `concept_environment_evolution_curriculum`
  difficulty-evolver must target the peak and stop short of the tail.
- The pressure-*type* result matters for habitat design: competitive vs selective pressure produce different
  emergent behaviour, not just different amounts.
