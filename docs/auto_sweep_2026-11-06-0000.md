## The root: the substrate authenticates no writes
`pheromone.annotate(task_ref, kind, u, c, agent=...)` takes `agent` as a **plain string from
the caller** and never verifies the writer is that agent. A cheater can post an annotation *as
anyone*:
- a fake warning from a respected agent, or a retraction of someone else's (now that
  `supersedes` is within-author, just impersonate the author);
- a fake gate verdict as another validator;
- inflated contributions under any name.

**Every identity-keyed guard sits on top of that unverified claim** — gate rotation and
no-self-review (`gates.py`), the anti-ping-pong window (`relay.py`), the reputation ledger
(`reputation.py`), the correlation de-duplication (`pheromone.read()`). The runs-400/401 fixes
(id normalisation, within-author supersede, contributor-excluded quorum) harden *accidental and
lazy* attacks; they don't stop a determined impersonator.

## Stated as the trust boundary
Written into `pheromone.py`'s docstring: the substrate is a **cooperative** coordination layer
that assumes a fleet one operator controls and does not impersonate itself. Closing the gap
needs one of: **signed writes** (operator-held key per agent), a **capability token** minted by
the pool (`executor_pool` already has `mint_token` / `redeem_token` — the nearest available
path), or an external identity provider.
