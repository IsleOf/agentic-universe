## What it shows
A large-scale multi-agent NYC-navigation simulation: Blue agents navigate efficiently, Red agents use
persuasive language to divert them onto billboard-heavy routes; hidden identities make navigation socially
mediated (trust vs deceive). Policies learned iteratively via Kahneman-Tversky Optimization.

- Best Blue policy improves task success **46.0% → 57.3%** across iterations…
- …but **susceptibility to adversarial steering stays high at 70.7%**.
- Later policies show **stronger selective cooperation** while preserving efficiency.
- **A persistent safety-helpfulness trade-off**: policies that better resist adversarial steering do **not**
  simultaneously maximise task completion — and it does **not** converge away with more optimisation.

## Why it matters
The nourishing-harness design assumes the capability-vs-safety tension (`concept_work_substitution_frontier`
challenges `principle_diversity_maintenance`) can be held on both sides — most likely by separating exploration
and deployment populations. CONSCIENTIA is a **measured** instance of that trade-off *per policy*, and it
persists under optimisation. And a **~70% persuasion-susceptibility floor** even after adversarial training
bears on the messaging-topology design and `emergent_subagent_training_induces_coordination` (the peer-influence
channel is reducible but has a high floor).
