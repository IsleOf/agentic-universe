## Three results
1. **Spontaneous emergence** of a universally adopted convention in a decentralized LLM-agent population
   (naming game), no explicit programming, no central coordination.
2. **Strong collective bias emerges during that process even when agents exhibit no bias individually** —
   a group-level distortion invisible in any single agent.
3. A **committed adversarial minority** past a critical mass can drive social change, imposing an
   alternative convention on the larger population.

## Why it matters
- **(2) is a fifth diversity-keystone erosion mechanism** — distinct from the four already listed (shared
  priors / shared evidence / interaction compression / emergent hubs). Convention *formation itself*
  manufactures collective bias, so an individually-unbiased, process-diverse population can still converge
  on a biased norm. Recorded as `challenges` `principle_diversity_maintenance`.
- **(3) makes norms both a design surface and an attack surface** — a committed minority as norm anchor is
  engineerable (`pattern_ostrom_swarm_governance`), but an adversarial minority in a free-interior swarm
  can capture the population's conventions, which `pattern_adversarial_agentic_monitor` must detect —
  norm-capture, not just individual misbehaviour. Recorded as `challenges` the monitor.
