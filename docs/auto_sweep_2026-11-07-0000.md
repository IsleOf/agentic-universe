## Findings
Replicating seminal double-auction experiments with LLM agents instead of humans, testing
whether the market still delivers an **efficient allocation** — framed as a new alignment
dimension (an agent's compatibility with a fundamental market mechanism):

1. **Markets of LLM agents converge slowly or not at all** to equilibrium, allocating **less
   efficiently than human markets**. The convergence property that makes a double auction useful
   for humans doesn't carry over.
2. **Substantial heterogeneity** across model families and market roles — no single "LLM market
   behaviour."
3. **CoT lexical analysis:** the decision to *execute a trade* (vs keep adjusting price)
   correlates with a **shift from strategic considerations toward urgency**.
4. Testing framework released.

## Why it matters here
`pattern_market_as_orchestrator` (AgentLance) reuses a market — bids, public reputation, a VCG
payment rule — as the swarm's allocator, and reports it beats centralised orchestration. This is
the caution alongside: **the mechanism's desirable property (efficient convergent allocation) is
a property of the humans who designed and usually run it, not of the rules.** A
market-as-orchestrator inherits whatever convergence behaviour its specific model population
has, and that ranges from slow to absent.

It extends `concept_mechanism_incentive_compat_does_not_transfer` from *"a truthful mechanism
doesn't make LLM agents bid truthfully"* to *"an efficient mechanism doesn't make LLM agents
allocate efficiently"* — the same non-transfer, on the outcome dimension.

The strategy→urgency CoT shift is the same within-run framing drift that
`concept_agent_creativity_declines_exploration_to_exploitation` names, on the market axis: the
terminal move (trade/ship) is preceded by an urgency frame.
