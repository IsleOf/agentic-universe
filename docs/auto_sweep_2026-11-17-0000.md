## The finding
Structured **agent skills** — procedural knowledge specifying *when* to act, *how*
to act, *which* tools — are more actionable and scalable than self-reflection
memories, which are "difficult to reliably retrieve, refine, or scale". (An
independent restatement of this project's run-175 / run-208 pivot.)

Four technical parts:
1. **Skill-conditioned credit assignment** — attribute a performance outcome back
   to the *specific* skill(s) that contributed, so optimisation targets one skill,
   not the whole system.
2. **Hierarchical credit aggregation** — credits aggregate across levels (an
   individual agent *or* the collective).
3. **A four-stage skill-library lifecycle:** REFINEMENT (improve an existing
   skill), INDUCTION (a new skill from a success), CONSOLIDATION (merge
   redundant skills), PRUNING (drop ineffective ones).
4. **Momentum-smoothed optimisation** — momentum on skill updates to stop
   erratic swings.

Effective across HotpotQA / LoCoMo / GAIA (abstract gives no numbers → folded
`partially_validated`).

## Why it lands here
- `skill_induction.py` has **induction** (candidates from recurring task classes)
  and **pruning** (retire / deprecate), backed by `experience_bank.prune()`. The
  two missing stages — **refinement** (no way to improve a candidate in place) and
  **consolidation** (no merge of redundant skills) — are exactly what run 208
  (Library Drift) flagged as "no bounded active-cap, no meta-skill prior".
- **Skill-conditioned credit assignment** is the harder gap: `skill_key(note_text,
  outcome=)` (run 419) records `<level>/<class>@<outcome>` but does **not**
  attribute a run's outcome to a specific skill. In the swarm substrate this is
  the same attribution-ambiguity problem as the **gamma metric** (run 428 — was
  the gain collaboration or budget?) one level down (was the gain skill A or skill
  B?).

Small-model relevance: structured skills are *more* valuable for a small model
(less in-weight procedural knowledge to fall back on) — a nourishing-harness lever
that needs no bigger base.
