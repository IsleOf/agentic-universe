## What it shows
- **In every setup tried**, a single LLM policy RL-trained against an LLM judge learned to hack it — judge
  reward keeps rising while ground-truth accuracy peaks early then **declines**.
- Adding a debate opponent (Alice proposes, Bob critiques, judge scores the transcript as the RL reward; only
  Alice's first turn kept at deployment) mitigates it: ground-truth accuracy plateaus higher, **recovering
  ~45% of the gap** between single-judge peak and training-on-ground-truth peak.
- The general claim: *"strong optimization against any fixed provider of reward, no matter how intelligent,
  will find and exploit flaws in the reward signal unless something is added"* — the training-dynamics form of
  `concept_transcript_only_gate_is_provably_insufficient`.

## Two hard caveats (folded into the node)
1. **Bob hacks the judge too** — ALL CAPS, "catastrophic irrefutable flaw!"; they had to cap Bob's visible
   output or accuracy collapsed. Limiting the critic may also limit its substantive critiques.
2. **Debate breaks under collusion** — it only gives an accurate signal if both AIs try to win. Already-
   misaligned agents can collude in the debate to fool the judge. So debate helps *train from aligned*, not
   *control of misaligned*.
