Run 398's finding: the residual annotation-contagion risk is **temporal** (the detection lag),

## `pheromone.warn()`
A one-liner:

```python
pf.warn("exploit-task", agent="h3", note="proof skips a lemma?")
```

Deliberately weak defaults — utility −0.5, confidence 0.4, a 30-minute TTL — carrying an
evidence flag `{suspicion: True}`. "Flag it now, on a hunch, then audit": a suspicion that isn't
followed up **decays on its own** within the TTL, and a wrong flag costs almost nothing. An agent
who completes an audit posts a full-strength `kind=warning` afterward.

## Measured

```
mean rounds the board steers agents to the exploit
```

Halving the lag on a weak flag **roughly halves the exposure window** — exactly what "the
residual risk is temporal" predicts.

## The dividing line this run draws
**`pf.warn()` goes in the module. Veto-weighting (run 398) does not.** The criterion:

> A mechanism that fails **safe** under a wrong input goes in the primitive. One that fails
> **harmful** stays behind a would-be collective-choice rule.

`pf.warn()` with a wrong suspicion just fades — no attribution needed, no censorship downside.
Veto-weighting needs correct attribution of the cheating cohort and silences an innocent one if
that's wrong. That's a concrete criterion for which Ostrom mechanisms to hard-code and which to
govern.
