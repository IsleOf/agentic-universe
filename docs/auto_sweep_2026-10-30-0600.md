**"Formal Verification of Agentic Systems over Operational Data"** (arXiv:2608.03609,
Mercado & Lomuscio, Imperial, 2026-08-04, VERIFIED).

## The formalism, and why it applies directly
A **STEAD** — *Stateful Tool-Enabled Agentic Deployment* — is **a single LLM plus a tool
orchestration harness acting over relational operational data.** That is this project's exact
shape, so the results apply rather than merely rhyme.

Specifications are **FO-CTL** over workflow execution and data evolution — *business-level*
requirements. The authors' explicit criticism of prior work is that it "mainly constrains or
analyses behaviour at the **agent's interface level**."

## The results are sobering
- Verification is **undecidable** in general.
- Under a **finite-domain restriction** plus a sufficient condition it is **PSPACE-complete**.
- The condition is **equivariance**: *renaming opaque identifiers in the data must
  correspondingly rename the selected tool calls.*
- **LLM-driven agents can violate this** — the model responds to the surface *form* of an
  identifier, not only its structural role.
- Their canonical deployment wrapper enforces equivariance for an arbitrary base agent, and
  computing its canonical representations is **graph-isomorphism-hard**.

## What it costs this project's own claims
Equivariance is a sharp test, and **several of this project's guards fail it**, because they
match on **names** rather than roles:

- `exec_guard`'s self-design perimeter is a literal path list plus keyword regexes. Rename or
  relocate a guarded file and the decision changes.
- The **run-371** destructive-key refusal — which I wrote two runs ago — matches substrings
  (`remove`, `delete`, `modify`, …). A patch key spelled **`rm_nodes`** or **`purge_nodes`**
  passes.

The tempting fix is to lengthen the word list. **That is the non-equivariant move repeated,
not a fix.** So instead of patching, I annotated both guards *in the code* with the scope of
what they actually provide:

> heuristics with good coverage against the accidental and the unsophisticated — **not verified
> properties**, and they should not be cited as such.

The real upgrade, named but not attempted: a **whitelist over patch shape** (add-only
structure) rather than a blacklist over key names.

## Why annotate rather than fix
Lengthening `_DESTRUCTIVE` would make the guard look stronger while leaving it exactly as
bypassable. Writing down what a guard does *not* guarantee is worth more than a longer list,
and it stops a future reader — including me — from treating the check as a proof.
