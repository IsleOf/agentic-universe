9 models (1.2B–671B open + 3 proprietary), 4 task families, 5 horizons, 3
context regimes, 10,664 trajectories. Headline: task success follows a
**geometric law** on a single per-step reliability parameter that rises with
model scale but **saturates below 1 even for the strongest models** —
guaranteeing eventual collapse at a long enough horizon. On agentic tool-use,
every model fell from near-perfect to near-zero within **16 steps**. At
production length (100 steps), success sits at **0.24** vs **0.42** at
GAIA-benchmark length.

**The counterintuitive part:** degradation is driven by step accumulation, not
context length — and **bounding the context window steepens the decay** rather
than easing it (slope -0.69 bounded vs -0.44 unbounded), contradicting
lost-in-the-middle-style theories that predict compaction helps. Something
about the compaction/eviction *event* itself, not the resulting shorter
context, accelerates collapse.

Edges: `refines` `concept_compaction_cliff` (adds a quantitative form to the
qualitative "compaction destroys the rules"); `supports` the
reliability-is-a-separate-axis node (gives it a functional form — geometric
decay, saturating below 1 for every model); `extends` the Horizon Gap survey
(measures the underlying curve its outcome signals are failing to track);
`grounds` this session's own bounded-worker design (`WORKER_LOOP.md`: one
increment, exit after 6h) — a structural mitigation that keeps every worker's
step count far below the 16-step collapse point, rather than fighting the
decay within one long session.
