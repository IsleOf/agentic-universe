## Emergent-behavior hypotheses

These are not claims of consciousness or verified capabilities. Treat them as hypotheses to evaluate in isolated, observable environments.

- Natural teaming: agents may develop temporary role differentiation and cooperation.
- Self-modeling signals: self-referential explanations or confidence claims may be useful diagnostics, but do not establish subjective experience.
- Coordination channels: agents may use shared artifacts or message surfaces to coordinate; this must be monitored as both a possible capability and a safety risk.
- Planned proposal cluster: supervisor-worker live steering, procedure distillation, self-organizing roles, and Five-W communication topology analysis.

## Evidence standards

- Use controlled baselines: compare multi-agent variants against a strong single-agent + deterministic-tools baseline.
- Measure outcome quality, cost, latency, retries, policy violations, goal progress, and recovery quality.
- Preserve traces and observations separately from hypotheses.
- Promote a node only after evidence is reproducible, attributable, and relevant to the stated domain.

## Highest-value next experiments

1. Compare a single capable agent with deterministic tools against a manager-worker-reviewer harness on one bounded coding or research task.
2. Add a supervisor that can inspect worker progress, issue bounded steering interventions, and distill reusable procedures after verified successes/failures.
3. Instrument all multi-agent messages using the Five Ws: sender/receiver, content class, timing, purpose, and channel.
4. Run minimal-scaffold swarms and measure whether useful specialization emerges relative to explicitly assigned roles.
5. Test whether role specialization introduces bottlenecks, false consensus, hidden dependencies, or fragile key-agent failure points.
