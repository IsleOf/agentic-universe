## The numbers (Table 1 — TAU-2 avg / BFCL-V4 multi-turn avg)

| model | TAU-2 | BFCL-V4 |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-235B-A22B | 56.5 | 52.0 |
| AgenticQwen-30B-A3B (3B active) | 53.7 | 50.2 |
| AgenticQwen-8B | 51.7 | 47.4 |
| **Qwen3-8B (vanilla)** | **19.3** | **23.8** |

An agentically-trained 8B reaches **~92%** of the 235B model on TAU-2. The *same* 8B
untrained reaches **~34%**. On XBench search, 30B-A3B scores 47.0 against the 235B's 48.0.

The authors attribute the residual gap to **context length** — the 8B/30B models support only
40K tokens — **not to reasoning capacity**.

## Why this is uncomfortable
A **32.4-point jump at fixed model size, from training**, in exactly the regime this project
targets. That is the sharpest small-model data point yet against the emergence-first /
no-weight-training fork, and it joins JIT-Agent and WHALE as the third member of the
trained-beats-frozen cluster.

**What it does not show:** the paper never tests *vanilla-8B plus a strong harness* — the
comparison this project actually cares about is simply absent. So it does not refute the
fork. But it establishes that harness-only would be starting from 19.3 with a long way to go,
and that is worth stating plainly rather than reading the result selectively.

## The constructive half
The residual bottleneck is **context length — a harness variable, not a weights variable.**
For a 40K-context local model, bounded curated retrieval stops being an optimisation and
becomes the lever on the binding constraint. That connects directly to two of this session's
own builds: `artifact_probe_memory_horizon_rig` (run 360 — bounded top-k beats unbounded past
a crossover) and `experience_bank`'s `top_k=3` default (run 357).
