Self-improving agents' search methods assume a *stationary* evaluation criterion — a fixed verifier or
benchmark that stays valid as the agent improves. RQGM's point: species adapt as their environments change
with them, and evaluation should be part of the improvement loop. The soundness mechanism is **controlled
utility evolution** — search organised into **epochs** with a fixed within-epoch criterion; the utility is
updated only at epoch boundaries, so self-improvement guarantees hold **per epoch**.

- adding a complementary agent-as-a-judge code-review signal beats prior SOTA on verifiable coding at
  1.35×–1.72× fewer tokens
- co-evolved writers reach 1.78×–1.86× higher acceptance under a diverse agent-as-a-judge panel;
  co-evolved graders reach **9% higher ground-truth accuracy**
- the strongest baseline reviewer over-accepts AI-generated papers at up to 1.91× the human rate; RQGM
  corrects this with an adversarial objective that discovers reviewers equally stringent on AI and human
  work

Bearing on the project: this is the **revision discipline** for `holdout_tasks.py`. The frozen 17-task set
is epoch *N*; a deliberate human edit (with a note) opens epoch *N+1*; held-out regression claims compare
only *within* an epoch, and sweep docs should record which epoch a run's baseline belongs to. Freezing
forever is one valid policy (never leave epoch *N*) — the risk it accepts is a stale criterion. RQGM's
anchored co-evolved grader (+9% ground truth) is the constructive complement to Who Grades the Grader's
always-pass collapse.
