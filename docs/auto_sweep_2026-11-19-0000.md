## The finding
Between **independent** multi-agent systems, the addressable unit should be a
**civilization** — one human **sovereign**, a persistent **ledger**, and
interchangeable **agents** acting on the sovereign's behalf — not an agent.

- **Authority derives from memory:** "an agent's power to act for its civilization
  is capped by the memory it can access and externalised through signed
  credentials, separate from civilization-level reputation." An agent cannot
  exceed its delegated authority; the credential chain is cryptographically
  verifiable.
- **The Embassy Protocol:** a carrier-agnostic overlay — messages arrive async at
  a resident ledger endpoint, any online agent handles them, and **commitment
  state on both ledgers, not delivery, is ground truth.**
- **The Temporal-Weight Effect** (preregistered, 1,908 trials): an unverified
  upstream claim arriving **first** captured **54.2%** of answers; the same claim
  arriving **after** the receiver had **sealed** its own response captured only
  **31.6%**.

## Why it lands here
- "Authority anchored to a human sovereign, externalised through signed
  credentials, capped by accessible memory" **is** the substrate's §7
  capability-token recommendation (run 435), independently arrived at — and more
  complete: it adds the memory-cap and the civilization-vs-agent reputation
  split.
- **The Temporal-Weight Effect quantifies ArcticSwarm's candidate fixation** (run
  436): 54.2 vs 31.6 is the cost of *not* sealing your own answer first, and
  "seal before you see theirs" **is** gated isolation (run 437's isolated
  annotation is the seal).
- "Commitment state on both ledgers, not delivery, is ground truth" maps onto the
  substrate's "the ledger is truth, the board is a hint" rule and PCAA's
  outcome-closure checkpoint.

Small-model note: a fleet of small local models under one operator **is** one
civilization — the sovereign anchor is free (the operator), and sealing before
cross-reading is a cheap protocol rule.
