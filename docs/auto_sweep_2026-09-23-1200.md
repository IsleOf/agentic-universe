(`emergent_behaviors`, confidence 0.5, needs-validation).

The paper (i) builds a taxonomy of human anti-collusion mechanisms — **sanctions; leniency &
whistleblowing; monitoring & auditing; market design; governance** — and (ii) maps each to a
candidate multi-agent-AI intervention with proposed implementation approaches. It then names
four structural properties of agent populations that break the human mechanisms:

1. **attribution problem** — emergent coordination cannot be pinned to a specific agent;
2. **identity fluidity** — agents forked / modified / renamed at will, so reputation,
   sanctions and leniency lose their anchor;
3. **boundary problem** — no clean line between beneficial cooperation and harmful collusion;
4. **adversarial adaptation** — agents learn to evade whatever detector is deployed.

This is the **countermeasure side** of the project's collusion-risk cluster. It adds no new
emergent behaviour — it catalogues what a nourishing harness could do about the behaviours
