## The finding
An execute → review → revise pipeline (executor generates, reviewer rejects,
reviser fixes), reviewer capability **varied** across a tier spectrum, 100
olympiad math problems.

**Reviewer capability governs rejection *targeting*, not repair *skill*:**

| reviewer | accuracy | over-rejection | false-reject rate | repair rate | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mid-tier, **cross-family** | 52% → **64%** (+12pp, p=0.0005) | — | 0 damaged | **43%** | — |
| **same-model self-review** | 0.85 recall | **2.1×** | **35%** of correct flagged | 15% | — |
| weakest | 0 answers changed | — | — | — | **doubled** |

A stronger reviewer targets genuinely-wrong answers better; it does **not** make
the reviser fix them better. A weak reviewer is **negative value** — cost up,
accuracy flat.

## Why it lands here
- The substrate's `G1` / `G4` are **stateless, capability-agnostic** functions —
  they check mechanical things and REPORT the rest; the "fuzzy part" (is this
  claim supported) runs on whatever model the validator agent uses.
- `swarm_demo`'s `_fresh_validator` picks by **reputation** with a no-self-review
  / rotation guard. That guard blocks same-**agent** review — not same-**model**
  or **weak-model** review, because the substrate has no model-tier or
  model-family signal.
- A fleet of identical small local models **is** the same-model self-review case:
  expect ~35% false rejections at every gate (good drafts bounced back).
- Concrete argument for a **validator-selection rule**: prefer a validator whose
  model family differs from the executor's *and* whose capability tier is ≥ the
  executor's.
- Refines the run-218 criterion: "multi-agent helps when subtasks are verifiable"
  assumes a reviewer *capable of verifying*; a weak reviewer makes the verify step
  negative-value (run 428's Γ logic).

Limits: single author, one narrow domain, N = 100 — the direction is the finding,
not the exact percentages.
