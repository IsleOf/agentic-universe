## The finding
- **Legibility** = how readable a trace appears to a human. **Interpretability** =
  the trace actually revealing the decision mechanism. A readable step may not be
  a load-bearing one.
- **Actual step importance** is operationalised as **advantage** — the change in
  expected reward from *including* that step, estimated by Monte Carlo rollouts (a
  causal ablation). **Judged importance** — an LLM judge or a fine-tuned step
  critic reading the text.
- **The gap is substantial:** capable LLMs beat a prevalence baseline but "fall
  well short of a noise ceiling"; step importance is "only partially recoverable
  from the text of the reasoning trace." Fine-tuning helps for *incorrect*
  responses, stays far from ceiling for *correct* ones.
- **Implications:** process reward models and LLM-as-judge frameworks may
  *misidentify* the truly important steps while appearing convincing.

## Why it lands here
The project **measures** trace legibility and leans on it as a monitorability
signal:
- `loop_metrics.trace_legibility` (run 155, threshold 0.70) — fraction of trace
  steps that read as sentences.
- `improvement_loop.verify_loop_edit(legibility=(before, after))` (run 156) —
  **rejects** an edit that trades legibility for held-out score.

This paper says that is a **floor**, not a ceiling:
- **Keep the floor.** An illegible trace is definitely not monitorable, and run
  156's caution (don't let the trace degrade) is directly supported.
- **Don't over-read it.** No doc or node should read a high `trace_legibility` as
  "the trace is interpretable / the reasoning is monitorable" — only "legible."
  Establishing that a step *matters* needs an advantage-style causal check (MC
  rollouts), which the project does not and cannot cheaply do offline.

It also sharpens `concept_cot_monitorability_is_a_fragile_property_to_preserve`
(preservation is necessary, not sufficient) and is the non-adversarial half of
`concept_rewriting_cot_alone_defeats_judges_90pct` (a judge misses which steps
matter even on an *honest* trace).
