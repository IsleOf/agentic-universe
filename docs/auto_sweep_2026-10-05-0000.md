## What it shows
Qwen 3.6 Plus + MiniMax M2.5 × Goose / OpenCode / OpenHands-SDK, 50-task Terminal-Bench Pro subset:

- **Harness choice induces up to a 40× difference in tokens per solved task**, while within-model pass-rate
  differences stay 0–8 pp. In this task regime the harness's biggest lever is *efficiency*, not pass rate.
- **Failure fingerprints replicate across models** — REASON-loop for Goose, VERIFY / MAX_TURNS for
  OpenHands-SDK, **IDLE-LOOP / TIME for OpenCode**. Each harness has a characteristic pathology that is largely
  *model-independent*.
- "no-action turns are a per-task **wait** tax, not just a token tax."

## Why it matters
- A harness component's failure mode is a property **of the component, not the model** — which is exactly why
  per-component gating (`concept_aggregate_score_masks_per_layer_regression`, run 115–116) and per-component
  evolution (the Phase 3 registry) are the right granularity.
- **OpenCode's real "idle-loop" pathology is independent evidence that the idle-agent probe
  (`artifact_probe_idle_agent_rig`) measures a real harness failure mode**, not just a hypothesis — a deployed
  harness genuinely gets stuck in no-action loops.
- The "wait tax" is an operational cost the token-economy view (§4) misses.
