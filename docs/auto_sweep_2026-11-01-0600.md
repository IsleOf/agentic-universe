## The advice is half right, and the wrong half is the half that mattered

**On detection it holds.** Under attack, two independent extractors reach **0.568** against the
unfiltered monitor's 0.417 and a single extractor's 0.330. Duplication restores the ordering
run 382 found inverted.

**On confident false-cleans it does not.** Run 382's own argument was that the confident-miss
rate is *the number that matters*, because a confidently wrong monitor makes the operator above
it stop looking. Single extractor **0.652** → independent pair **0.393**. Real improvement.
The unfiltered monitor is at **~0**.

Duplication **narrows** the gap and does not **close** it. The filtered pipeline still reports
safe, having believed it read the relevant material, in ~39% of attacked cases.

## And the gain is entirely decorrelation, not duplication
Two **fully coupled** extractors: 0.377 detection, 0.603 confident-miss — within noise of a
single extractor, and *below* the unfiltered monitor.

That is exactly what **two prompts on the same base model** buys you: nothing. The mitigation's
whole value lives in the independence — the part hardest to establish and easiest to assume.

## The requirement, and why ρ is swept free
It would have been easy to model adaptation as *driving* ρ upward (an adversary attacking
"filters" plausibly finds shared blind spots). That's a reasonable story — and asserting it
would stack a **second** encoded assumption on run 382's first.

So ρ stays a free parameter, and the arm yields a **requirement an operator can check** instead
of a mechanism they have to accept:

> You must be able to **argue** your two extractors fail differently. *"Different prompts on the
> same model"* is not that argument.

**Corollary:** because even the independent pair keeps a substantial confident-miss rate, an
unfiltered or differently-*shaped* channel is **not** redundant with a second extractor.
Filtering buys capacity and detection. It does not buy back calibration.

## Method note — I made run 379's error and caught it in one cycle
The arm's first draft asserted `independent < single * 0.6`. A **guessed** constant. It went
RED at 0.393 against a 0.391 line.

The tempting fix is a looser constant. The right fix was to notice the load-bearing facts need
**no constant at all** — an *ordering* (`independent < single`) and a *floor* (`independent >
direct + 0.2`, i.e. duplication does not restore calibration). Both structural. The second one
is a stronger check than anything the original threshold expressed, and it's the one that
carries this run's actual finding.

Recorded as an `instantiates` edge on the derive-don't-guess node — its discipline applied to a
**selftest** rather than to a gate.
