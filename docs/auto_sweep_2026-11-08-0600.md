## Increment
Literature. Folded **arXiv:2609.05279 — "Testing Interchangeability in LLM Agent Teams"** (Gao,
Yu, Deng, Li, Wang; 2026-09-04). It directly prices something the swarm substrate assumed was
free.

## The test
Eight teams per setting formed independently from **one base model** on the same tasks, each
agent keeping a private notebook across ten formation episodes. Then **role-matched agents are
traded between teams** and held-out performance measured — against a **placebo** that reproduces
the disruption of a roster change without changing who's in the seat.

## Findings
1. A swap costs **little in task score** but raises **communication-per-progress by 16–63%**.
2. In Hanabi a **swapped agent is more expensive than an inexperienced one** — active
   interference from conventions learned with its *former* partner, not mere inexperience.
3. In Collab-Overcooked, when the **agenda-setter** is replaced, most of the extra communication
   comes from **the agent that stayed** — the incumbent pays the adaptation cost.
4. The swap penalty tracks **how far independently-formed teams drift apart**: greedy decoding
   lowers both; doubling formation history raises both.
5. Recommendation: agents are more fungible in **task outcome** than in **coordination
   efficiency** — prioritise coordination efficiency in roster management; team stability yields
   efficiency gains.

## Why it lands on the substrate
The design's core stance — *roles are transient computational functions, released when the task
ends* (§5.4) — and `swarm_demo`'s **fresh-eyes rule** deliberately rotate agents per stage and
per gate. **This paper says that rotation is not free: 16–63% more coordination overhead,
growing with how long the role was held.**

The substrate's rotation buys **audit validity** (no self-review) and the paper's cost is
**communication overhead, not correctness** — so this is a **measured tradeoff to record, not a
refutation**. But it says:

- rotate for safety where safety needs it (the gates), let stable working pairs persist
  elsewhere;
- expect the **stayer**, not the newcomer, to carry the re-coordination cost when the
  personal-assistant role churns.

And it reframes `emergent_social_conventions`: usually a capability, here also a **liability on
transfer** — a convention learned with a former partner *interferes* with the new team's
patterns.
