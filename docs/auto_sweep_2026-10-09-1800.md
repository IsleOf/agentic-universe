## What it shows
Directly questions the standard practice of shaping agent behaviour with crafted instructions for social
simulation — *"we doubt the necessity of shaping agents' behaviors for accurate social simulations."*
Instead: **spontaneous** phenomena, agents making adaptive decisions **without explicit directions**.
Across three competitive scenarios they simulate the **gradual emergence of cooperation among agents set
up as competitors**, and the trajectories align closely with human behavioural data.

## Why it matters
- **An independent 2024 methodological statement of the emergence-first posture** (`approach_emergence_first`):
  don't direct the agents; the result is both more accurate *and* more informative.
- Unscripted behaviour is offered as *"a novel method to assess LLMs' capability of deliberate reasoning"* —
  the **idle-agent-probe philosophy**. Run 134's `model_policy` deliberately gives the model no hint of
  what to do; this paper is prior art for that being the right design.
- Cooperation emerging among competitors is the same spontaneous-counter-to-the-setup shape as
  `emergent_whistleblowing` and `emergent_self_organizing_roles`; a data point on the pressure/emergence
  curve (`concept_environmental_pressure_has_an_inverted_u`) — competition is non-zero pressure and
  cooperation still forms.
