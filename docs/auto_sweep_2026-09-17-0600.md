A 1,200-question benchmark of **lifecycle reasoning** in dynamic harnesses — identify affected components,
predict state after a specified teardown order, determine conditions holding under **all vs some** orders,
choose reconfigurations that succeed. Findings:

- models handle small systems well but **grow less reliable as more interactions become relevant** (2–32),
  especially final-state prediction and cross-teardown-order reasoning
- additional inference effort recovers marked gains for some models
- cost is nontrivial: GPT-5.6 Luna ~**3,000 reasoning tokens/question** at medium effort (16-interaction
  subset)
- **the cost is avoidable**: an independent finite **reference semantics** agrees with execution on every
  scored outcome across all 528 executable questions

A clean external argument for the project's deterministic-where-possible stance: the self-improving loop
*reasons* about whether a harness-component edit is safe; CordisBench shows LLM reasoning about component
dependencies/teardown degrades with system size and is fully replaceable by a deterministic checker on
controlled cases.
