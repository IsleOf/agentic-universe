## What it actually does
**MARP** — Multi-Agent Reward Prediction — extends preference-based reward modelling to
multi-agent RL. Instead of handcrafted per-agent rewards it learns a **shared** reward model
from **episode-level evaluations of collective outcomes**, letting decentralised agents align
with global social objectives.

Studied in the **Harvest Game** (canonical sequential social dilemma over a common-pool
resource). The headline: **by modifying only the high-level evaluation metric**, the same
framework aligns behaviour with **sustainability, equality, or peace** — and with composites
of individual and group-level objectives — inside one training regime.

## Read the title carefully
*"Why Study Emergent Behavior When You Can Regulate It?"* is a rhetorical hook. The paper's
actual claim is narrower: emergence can be treated as a **target of regulation** *as well as* a
phenomenon to analyse. Nobody argues it shouldn't be studied.

**Folding the title rather than the abstract would have manufactured a much sharper challenge
to this project than the work supports.** That is worth naming as a third member of a pattern:
runs 362 and 370 caught *search summaries* attributing claims that weren't in the papers; this
is *source framing* that overstates. A title is the part of a paper most optimised for
attention, and it is the part a fold is most tempted to quote.

The authors' own limitation, carried rather than dropped: *"the present empirical validation is
limited to a single environment, and we therefore present MARP as a proof of concept within the
studied domain."* Confidence set to **0.45** accordingly.
